# After a mistake

Everyone who uses a phone long enough makes a mistake. A tap that should have waited. A code read aloud. A file pasted into the wrong box. The shame is useful only if it makes you slow down *next* time. Right now, speed and a clear order matter more than blame.

You are not the first person this happened to. The national helpline **1930** is free and takes these calls all day.

---

## Money went to the wrong person, or to a scammer

Minutes count. Banks can sometimes hold a payment if they hear while it is still moving.

1. Call your bank on the number printed on the back of your card — not a number from the chat, and not the person who just took the money.
2. Tell them it is unauthorised. Ask them to freeze the account if needed, and to try to stop or recall the payment. You may hear words like “chargeback” (asking the payment network to reverse a card payment) or “lien/freeze.” You do not need the vocabulary. You need them to act.
3. File a complaint at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or call **1930**, ideally within 24 hours.
4. Write down, without editing: the amount, the time, the UPI ID or account you sent to, and which app you used. Screenshots help. Do not crop out the ugly parts.

Do not send a second payment to “unlock,” “reverse,” or “recover” the first one. That is a second scam, using the first as bait.

---

## You shared an OTP or PIN

1. Call the bank immediately. Ask them to block the card or account.
2. Change your UPI PIN from inside the official UPI app, once you are sure the phone is safe to use.
3. Look at recent transactions for anything you did not do.
4. Report at cybercrime.gov.in or **1930**.

Hang up on the person who asked for the code. They will not help you undo it.

---

## You clicked a suspicious link or installed something

1. Turn on airplane mode, or switch off Wi-Fi and mobile data, so the app cannot keep talking to someone else.
2. Uninstall the app. If you cannot, stay offline and get help from a person you trust, on a different device if you can.
3. Change passwords for email and banking from a phone or computer you trust — not from inside the suspicious app.
4. Check recent transactions.
5. If you gave someone remote control of the screen (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, “Quick Support”), treat the phone as unsafe. Back up photos, then consider a factory reset (erasing the phone and setting it up again).

---

## A photo or video of you was shared without your consent

1. Use the platform’s **Report** button (WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook).
2. Screenshot the *address* of the post (the URL) as evidence before it disappears. You do not need to forward the image itself.
3. Contact [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or **1930**.
4. If the content is sexual, this can be a serious crime. Contact the police. You did not cause this by existing in a photograph.

Do not pay someone who says they will take it down. Do not send more pictures “to settle this.”

---

## You pasted work or customer data into an AI tool by mistake

1. Delete the conversation if the tool lets you.
2. Tell your manager sooner rather than later. Early honesty is easier to repair than a client finding out first.
3. If the tool has a way to request deletion of data, use it. Deleting the chat on your screen is not always the same as deleting it on their computers — but it is still the first step.

---

## General reminder

Careful people get caught too. The worst extra damage is usually delay. Act, then tell one trusted person so you are not carrying the next steps alone.
