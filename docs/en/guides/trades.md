# Plumbers and electricians

The customer wants a quotation this evening. You have notes in the workshop book: two taps, a length of pipe, an hour of labour. You could type that into a chatbot — ChatGPT or Gemini, programs that turn rough notes into a polite message — and send something that looks like a company letter. Leave the customer’s name, phone, and house out of the paste. Put your real prices in yourself.

Use AI to communicate clearly, prepare drafts faster, and explain your work — while keeping customer information and payments safe.

---

## A useful learning path

1. **Write a quotation.** Ask AI to turn your list of materials and labour into a clear quotation. Replace the customer's real name, phone number, and address with placeholders such as [Customer] and [Area].
2. **Explain the work.** Ask for a simple explanation of the repair that a customer can understand — why the MCB tripped, why the tap still drips — without photographing their bathroom or fuse box in a way that shows keys, layouts, or the street.
3. **Translate a message.** Draft the message without private details, translate it, and read it once before sending. A polite phrase in English can sound cold, or oddly intimate, in another language.
4. **Check every payment yourself.** Never trust a screenshot. Open your own bank or **UPI** app — UPI is the system behind GPay, PhonePe, and BHIM — and confirm that the money arrived. A screenshot is a picture. Anyone can make a picture.

---

## Try this prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool:

> Turn these notes into a polite quotation. Separate materials and labour. Use simple language. Do not add prices or promises that I did not provide: [paste notes without customer details]

---

## Payments and other traps

A customer who says “I have paid, see the screenshot, start the work” has not paid until *your* app shows the credit. The same is true of a QR code they ask you to scan “to receive” money: receiving does not use your UPI PIN. If someone wants your PIN, they are sending money *out*.

Callers who claim to be the bank, the platform, or “GST department” and want an **OTP** — a one-time password, the short SMS code — are not those people. Hang up. Use the number on the back of your card.

AI produces a draft. You remain responsible for checking prices, measurements, safety requirements, and promises made to the customer. If the tool adds a warranty you do not offer, delete it before you send.

---

## Never share with AI

- A customer's phone number or home address
- Aadhaar, PAN, bank, card, OTP, or UPI PIN details
- Photos showing keys, access codes, security systems, or private rooms
- A payment screenshot as proof that money arrived

The quotation can be elegant. The house key in the background of a “before” photo cannot be taken back once it is on someone else’s server.
