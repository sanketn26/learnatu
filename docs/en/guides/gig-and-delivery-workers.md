# Gig and delivery workers

You are between orders. A WhatsApp message arrives with a company logo: your account will be paused unless you complete verification, pay a small onboarding fee, or tap a link and share the screen “so support can fix it.” The shift is already tight. That hurry is the scam’s favourite weather.

Gig and delivery work — Swiggy, Zomato, Porter, Rapido, and similar apps — runs on your phone. **AI** here means chatbots such as ChatGPT or Gemini: you type a message, they rewrite or translate it. They are useful for wording. They are a poor place to put a customer’s address, your live location, or a payslip.

Use them for communication and planning. Keep employer, customer, location, and payment data private.

---

## Useful tasks

A support chat is going in circles and you want one clear paragraph. Paste a version with the order number and the customer’s name taken out, and ask the tool to make it polite and short. Put the real details back in the official app before you send.

A customer wrote in a language you read slowly. Translate the *message*, not the address book. Remove the name, phone, and flat number first. Read the translation once. Then reply in the app, not in a private tool that still holds their details.

A new contract or platform policy is long. Ask for a list of questions you should take to a colleague or to official support — “What happens if I refuse a trip after reaching the pin?” — without uploading the PDF that has your ID number on it.

Monthly expenses are a mess. You can ask how to group fuel, data, and food into categories. Do not upload bank statements, UPI screenshots, or the CSV from the partner app. **UPI** is the system behind GPay, PhonePe, and BHIM that sends money from your phone.

---

## Try this prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool:

> Rewrite this as a short, polite message to in-app support. Keep every fact I wrote. Do not invent an order number, a promise, or a threat to leave the platform: [paste your notes with names, phones, and addresses removed]

---

## Common risks

Fake onboarding fees, “task” jobs that pay only after you send money, account-verification links in WhatsApp, screen-sharing requests, and callers who want an **OTP** — a one-time password, the short code a bank or app texts you — are the usual set.

**KYC**, or Know Your Customer, is the identity check platforms use: a photo of your Aadhaar or driving licence, done *inside* their own app. A link in a message that says “complete KYC to keep earning” is often a trap to steal that photo.

Open the official worker app yourself for support. Do not call back the number that called you. Do not install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or “Quick Support” because an unexpected caller asked you to. That lets them see your screen, including the UPI PIN box.

If someone already took money, call your bank on the number on the card and report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or **1930**. Do not send a second payment to “unlock” the first.

---

## Never share with a public AI tool

- Customer names, addresses, phone numbers, or order details
- Your live location, or a screenshot of the map with the pin still on it
- IDs, Aadhaar, driving licence, or KYC photos
- Payslips, bank statements, or UPI screenshots
- Employer-only information: internal rates, codes, or messages marked for partners only
- OTPs, PINs, or passwords

The draft of a message can live in the chatbot. The person who ordered dinner, and the money that pays your fuel, should not.
