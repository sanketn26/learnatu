# Homemakers and family caregivers

The school circular is in English you have to read twice. The electricity bill has a line you have never seen. Someone in the house is unwell, and a WhatsApp forward is already offering a spice and a diagnosis. You are the person who translates the household to the outside world, and the outside world back in.

AI — chatbots such as ChatGPT or Gemini, which write and translate in ordinary language — can help with household communication, learning, planning, and understanding unfamiliar information. It should not make family health, financial, or safety decisions. It has not met your child, opened your passbook, or examined anyone.

---

## Useful tasks

A school or society message is long. Copy it into a note, take out the child’s name, the admission number, and the flat number, then ask the tool to explain it in simpler language or another language you use at home. Put the real names back only in the reply you send to the teacher or the secretary.

A shopping list can become a rough weekly plan: what to buy once, what can wait. Check prices with the actual seller. The chatbot does not know this week’s onion rate in your market.

A bill or a form uses a word you do not trust yourself to guess — “arrears,” “nominee,” “co-pay.” Ask for a plain-language explanation of the *term*, not a decision about whether to pay. Confirm official requirements on that organisation’s own website, the one you type, not a link from a message.

Before a parent-teacher meeting, a bank visit, or a clinic appointment, ask the tool for a list of questions you might forget in the room. You still ask them. You still listen to the human answer.

A complaint to a shop or a society office can be drafted as a polite paragraph. Leave account numbers, meter numbers, and Aadhaar out of the paste. You can add the number in the letter you actually send.

---

## Try this prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool:

> Explain this notice in simple language. List anything I should check with the school / bank / clinic. Do not tell me to change medicine, pay extra, or share documents. I have already removed names and numbers: [paste the stripped text]

---

## Stay safe

Do not upload family identity documents, medical records, children’s photographs, school details, addresses, bills containing account numbers, or private conversations. A photo of a report card or a prescription is still those things, even if you only wanted “what does this mean?”

Check prices with the actual seller, health information with a qualified professional, and official requirements on the relevant organisation's website.

If a call claims to be the bank or the school and wants an **OTP** — a one-time password, the short code that arrives by SMS — hang up. Call back on a number you already have. Nobody genuine needs that code read aloud.

---

## Never share with a public AI tool

- Aadhaar, PAN, passports, or photos of those cards
- Medical reports, prescriptions, or a list of medicines a family member takes
- Children’s photographs, full names, school names, or class sections
- Home addresses, landmark-heavy photos of the door, or society gate codes
- Bills or passbooks that show account numbers
- Private family chats, voice notes, or arguments you wanted “help understanding”

The circular can be explained without the child still being in it. That is the whole trick.
