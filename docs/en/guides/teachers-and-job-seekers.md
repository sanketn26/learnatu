# Teachers and job seekers

It is late. Tomorrow’s class still needs a worksheet, or the job portal wants a one-page résumé by morning. A chatbot — ChatGPT or Gemini, programs that write in ordinary language — will produce something that looks finished. That is the risk as well as the help: it can sound like you when it is not, and it will happily hold a student’s marks or your Aadhaar if you paste them.

AI can help prepare drafts and practise skills. It should not impersonate your work or receive other people's private information.

---

## For teachers

You can ask for example questions using fictional students — “Meera in Class 7,” not the real Meera whose parent is in the WhatsApp group. You can paste a general instruction (no names) and ask for a simpler reading level, then check that the meaning did not flip.

Brainstorming activities is a fair use. Accuracy and suitability are still yours: is this right for *this* board, *this* age, *this* classroom? Chatbots invent facts with a textbook’s confidence. Check against the syllabus you actually teach. **NCERT**, the organisation behind many Indian school textbooks, publishes at [ncert.nic.in](https://ncert.nic.in); start there rather than trusting a citation the tool made up.

Never upload student work, names, grades, accommodations, or contact details without approval. A photograph of answer papers is still a stack of children’s writing. A “please mark this” paste into a personal account can break the trust parents placed in the school.

---

## For job seekers

Improve a résumé after replacing personal details with placeholders: city instead of a full address, “email@example.com” instead of your real one. Practise interview questions out loud. Draft a cover letter from *your* real experience, then cut anything the tool added that you did not do.

Verify every employer independently. Open their website yourself. Call a number you find there, not the number in the WhatsApp broadcast. Never pay to unlock a job, interview, task income, or equipment shipment. A fee before work is the usual shape of a fake listing.

Do not let AI invent qualifications, employers, achievements, or references. A fluent lie is still a lie, and the interview will ask you to live inside it.

---

## Try this prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool.

For a teacher:

> Suggest five practice questions on this topic for Class 8, using fictional student names. Do not invent syllabus facts. If you are not sure something is in the chapter, say so: [topic only, no real student work]

For a job seeker:

> Turn these notes about my real experience into a short cover letter. Do not add employers, degrees, tools, or achievements I did not list. Use placeholders for address and phone: [your notes]

---

## Never share with a public AI tool

- Student names, photos, marks, answer sheets, or home contact details
- A child’s disability or extra-support notes, unless your institution has approved a specific tool for that
- Your Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, or a photograph of certificates with numbers still visible
- A company’s internal job sheet or another applicant’s data
- OTPs or logins for the school portal or a job site

Turn off ChatGPT’s “Improve the model for everyone” if you use a personal account. That setting lets the company use chats to train — teach — its software. Student work and job applications do not belong in that stream.
