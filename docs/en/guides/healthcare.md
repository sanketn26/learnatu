# Doctors and nurses

AI can help draft general communication and explain complex language. It must not replace clinical judgment, approved systems, or patient consent.

## A safe learning path

1. **Use fictional examples.** Remove names, dates, identifiers, contact details, images, and unusual facts that could identify a patient.
2. **Draft general instructions.** Use AI for a general-language template, then check it against your organisation's approved guidance.
3. **Translate cautiously.** Have a qualified person verify medication, dosage, consent, discharge, and emergency wording.
4. **Verify medical claims.** Check authoritative clinical sources and local protocols. Do not rely on citations generated by AI.

## Try this prompt

> Rewrite this general, non-patient-specific instruction in plain language for an adult reader. Preserve every warning. If anything is ambiguous, list it instead of guessing: [approved general text]

## Never share with a public AI tool

- Patient names, images, reports, prescriptions, IDs, or contact details
- Appointment records or combinations of facts that identify someone
- Login details or content from a restricted clinical system
- Information your organisation has not approved for external processing

Never use an AI answer alone to diagnose, prescribe, triage, calculate a dose, or make an emergency decision.
