# Voice, images, and file uploads

Typing is not the only way in. You can speak to some AI tools. You can send a photo of a bill. You can attach a PDF. That feels like showing a helpful person a piece of paper across a table.

It is not a table. It is a copy leaving your phone.

An **upload** means the file — or the picture, or the recording — is sent to the company’s computers so the tool can “see” it. Once it is there, you cannot be sure who will store it, for how long, or whether a person on their side could ever read it.

## Before you upload anything

Walk around the picture with your eyes the way you would before posting in a family WhatsApp group.

- Crop out faces, house numbers, signatures, QR codes (those square barcodes), and account numbers
- Look at the background: a school uniform, a nameboard, a medicine strip, a calendar with an appointment
- If the tool lets you, remove extra hidden information some files carry (sometimes called metadata) — or better, retype the small bit you actually need
- Prefer a made-up sample: “Name: A. Kumar, Amount: ₹0” instead of a real invoice
- If this is a work phone, check whether your employer allows the tool at all

Voice is easy to forget. A recording can catch a child’s name in the next room, a health detail you said without thinking, or the sound of where you are. Speak only what you would be willing to type.

## What must never go into a public AI tool

Identity documents (Aadhaar, PAN, passport), medical records, customer lists, private contracts, intimate images, keys, access codes, photos of a lock or a security panel, bank statements, or a screenshot of an OTP.

If the file would embarrass you, endanger someone, or hand a stranger a way into money or a house, it does not belong in the chat.

## A realistic moment

Farhan wanted a clearer explanation of a hospital bill. He photographed the whole page, including the patient’s full name, age, diagnosis, and the hospital ID. The explanation he got was useful. The photograph, with all of that still on it, was now on a company’s server.

He could have covered the name with his thumb, or typed one confusing line of charges with the name removed. Same help. Far less given away.
