# How to check an AI answer

AI can invent facts, sources, prices, rules, quotations, and phone numbers. It does not put a warning on the invented parts. The sentence that is true and the sentence that is made up arrive in the same calm tone.

So “it sounded sure” is not a check. Confidence is a writing style.

## Use the three-check method

1. **Check the claim.** Circle, in your head, anything that would cause harm if it were wrong: a name, a date, a price, a law, a medicine, a helpline, a historical fact for homework. Those are the parts that must not stay on trust.
2. **Check the source.** Open the official website, the original document, the textbook, or a person whose job it is to know. Type the address yourself. Do not use a link the AI invented.
3. **Check the fit.** Even a true statement may not apply to *you*. A rule for another country, a fee that changed last year, a medical note written for a different age or condition — all of these can be “correct” in general and dangerous in your case.

Do not ask the same AI “Are you sure?” and treat a second confident yes as proof. It may repeat the same error with more adjectives.

## What this looks like in real life

You ask for the number to report cybercrime in India. The tool replies with a number and a sentence that sounds governmental.

The check: open [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) yourself, or call **1930**, which is the number this handbook uses because it is the official national helpline. If the AI’s number does not match, throw the AI’s number away. Do not “try it anyway.”

The same pattern works for a school date, a bank process, a medicine name, or a clause in a form. The tool drafted. You verified.
