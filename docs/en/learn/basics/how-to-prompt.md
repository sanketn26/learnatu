# How to ask AI a useful question

Talking to an AI is not a spell. You do not need special technical words. You need to say, in ordinary language, what you want, for whom, and what it must not invent.

A **prompt** is the message you type (or speak) to the tool. The clearer that message, the less the tool has to guess — and guessing is how it makes things up.

## A simple formula

You can copy this and fill in the brackets:

> Help me **[do the task]** for **[who will read it, or the situation]**. Use **[language and tone]**. Include **[what must be there]**. Do not **[the boundary]**. If you would have to invent a fact, ask me instead.

That last line matters. Without it, the tool will often fill gaps with confident fiction — a price you never quoted, a date you never set, a guarantee you never made.

## An example from ordinary work

A plumber has handwritten notes: two taps, one hour of labour, no prices yet. Instead of pasting the customer’s name and address, they write:

> Turn these material and labour notes into a short customer quotation. Use polite, simple English. Separate materials and labour. Do not invent prices, dates, or guarantees. Replace any personal information with placeholders.

The AI returns a draft. The plumber puts the real prices in. The customer never had their address sitting in a chat box.

## How to improve the answer

You can keep talking. You do not have to start again.

- “Make this shorter.”
- “Explain that word.”
- “Give me three alternatives.”
- “What did you assume?”
- “List what I should check before I send this.”

That last request is one of the most useful. It reminds you that the draft is not finished work.

## Before you press send

Read your prompt once as if a stranger will see it — because, in a public tool, that is closer to the truth than it feels.

Remove names, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, patient details, children’s school information, and anything your workplace would not put on a public noticeboard.

You are not being paranoid. You are keeping the useful part (the *kind* of task) and leaving the private part (the *whose*) on your side of the screen.
