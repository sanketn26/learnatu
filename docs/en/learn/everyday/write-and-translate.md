# Write and translate messages with AI

You know what you want to say. The words will not sit still — or they need to sit in another language. That is a fair job for AI: turn rough notes into a polite message, or carry the meaning into Telugu, Hindi, English, or another language you asked for.

It is a draft. You still send it.

## Safe steps

1. **Strip the private parts first.** Take out names, phone numbers, addresses, payment details, and anything you would not put on a postcard. You can put them back after the draft exists.
2. **Say who it is for.** “A short WhatsApp message to a customer who is waiting for a delivery,” is better than “make this professional.”
3. **Say what not to add.** “Do not invent a promise, a date, a discount, or a medical claim.” Otherwise the tool will often “help” by adding one.
4. **Read the result as if you are the other person.** Would you be comfortable sending this? Does every number still match your notes?
5. **For high-stakes wording, ask a human.** Medication, consent, a contract, an emergency instruction, or a legal notice should be checked by someone who actually speaks that language well — not only by the tool.

## Try this prompt

> Rewrite this as a short, respectful WhatsApp message in Telugu. Keep every fact and number I wrote. Do not add promises. After the translation, explain it back to me in English so I can see what it now says: [safe text]

That last request — “explain it back” — is how you catch a translation that flipped a meaning. “Do not pay until you inspect the goods” and “pay now, inspect later” are a few words apart and a lawsuit apart.

## Why translation needs a second look

Languages do not line up word for word. A polite phrase in English can sound cold, or oddly intimate, in another language. A medical instruction can lose a negative (“do not”) and become the opposite.

Never rely on an unchecked AI translation for consent, medication, dosage, contracts, emergencies, or safety instructions. For a birthday wish to an aunt, a small error is embarrassing. For a discharge note from a hospital, it can be dangerous.
