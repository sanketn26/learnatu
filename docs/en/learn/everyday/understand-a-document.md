# Understand a difficult document

A form arrives. A rental clause. A school circular. A loan’s “terms and conditions.” Half the words feel like they were written for someone else.

AI can explain unfamiliar language. Uploading the *whole* document can also hand over names, account numbers, signatures, and the other party’s private terms.

You do not have to choose between “understand nothing” and “upload everything.”

## A safer method

1. Find the small paragraph or the single term that is blocking you. You do not need the whole ten pages in the chat.
2. Copy only that general wording. Take out names, numbers, addresses, signatures, and anything that identifies a person or a company.
3. Ask for a plain-language explanation **and** a list of questions to ask a human. That second part keeps you from treating the explanation as a decision.
4. Put the explanation next to the original sentence. If they do not match, trust the original until a qualified person says otherwise.
5. Before you sign, pay, or refuse, ask the person whose job it is: the teacher, the bank staff, a lawyer, a union, a trusted business owner.

AI has not read the rest of the agreement, does not know your situation, and may not know the current law where you live.

## Try this prompt

> Explain this general clause in simple language. Tell me what it appears to require, what is unclear, and which questions I should ask a qualified professional. Do not tell me whether to sign it: [non-private clause]

Notice what you are *not* asking: “Should I sign?” That is a decision with consequences the tool cannot carry for you.

## A realistic moment

Meera’s landlord sent a two-page addendum. She almost photographed both pages into an AI tool. Instead she typed one sentence, with the building address removed: the bit about “vacating within seven days of notice.” The tool explained that this might be unusually short and listed questions to ask. She took those questions to a neighbour who had rented for years, then to the landlord. She still used AI. She did not give away the rest of the contract to do it.
