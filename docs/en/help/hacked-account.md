# My account may be hacked

“Hacked” here means someone else can get in: they posted as you, they changed the password, or you see logins you did not make. Use a phone or computer you trust — not a public café computer, and not a phone that still has a suspicious app on it.

1. Open the official app, or type the website address yourself. Do not use a link from a message that “helps you recover” the account.
2. Change the password to a new one you have not used anywhere else. A short sentence is stronger than a name and a year.
3. Sign out all other sessions and remove unknown devices. In many apps this sits under Security or Devices. It kicks the other person out.
4. Check the recovery email, recovery phone number, forwarding rules (secret copies of your mail going elsewhere), and connected apps. Attackers often plant a back door here so they can walk back in.
5. Turn on **two-step verification** — a second check after the password, such as a code on your phone — and save the backup codes on paper, not in the same inbox.
6. Warn friends or colleagues if the account sent strange requests. A short “this was not me” saves the next person.

If you cannot sign in, use only the platform’s official recovery page. People in comments and ads who offer to “recover your account for a fee” are often the second scam.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Trust anyone replying on social media offering to “recover” your account.
    - Use a link or number they send instead of the platform’s official recovery page.
    - Leave your recovery email or phone number unchecked when several accounts may be affected.

If several accounts might be involved, secure email first. That is the key that resets the others.
