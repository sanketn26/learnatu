# I sent money or shared an OTP

Stop arguing with the person who took it. They will keep you on the line so you do not call the bank. Hang up. The next ten minutes are for official numbers only.

An **OTP** is the short code a bank or app texts you to approve one action. If you read it out or typed it into a page you did not open yourself, treat the account as already in someone else’s hands until the bank says otherwise.

1. Contact your bank or payment app through the official app, or the number printed on the back of your card.
2. Ask them to secure the account and record the fraudulent transaction. Give the amount, time, and UPI ID or account number if you have them.
3. In India, call **1930** promptly and submit a report at **cybercrime.gov.in**.
4. Save transaction IDs, phone numbers, messages, and screenshots. Do not edit them to look neater. Do not delete the chat “in anger.”
5. Change affected passwords from a phone or computer you trust.
6. Tell one person you trust, so the next calls are not all on you.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Pay a “recovery agent” or send another payment to unlock, reverse, or refund the first one.
    - Edit or delete transaction IDs, messages, or screenshots before reporting.
    - Wait to report — call **1930** and contact your bank immediately.

If you shared a UPI PIN, card PIN, password, or recovery code, tell the bank or the app exactly what was exposed. They cannot guess which door is still open.
