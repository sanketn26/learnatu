# Fake links and fake apps

You get a message. There is a button. The logo looks like your bank. The language sounds like a bank. One tap can open a page that is not your bank at all — and if you type an OTP or a password there, the money can leave.

Fraudsters build websites and apps that copy the real ones. You are not foolish if you almost tapped. You are the person they designed it for.

A **link** is the address of a page, the way a house has an address. On a phone it often hides behind words like “Click here.” An **app** is a program on your phone. A fake one can look identical on the home screen.

---

## How to spot a fake link

1. **Read the full address, slowly.** `hdfc-bank-refund.in` is not HDFC Bank. The real site is closer to `hdfcbank.com`. Extra words, extra hyphens, and a different ending (`.net`, `.xyz`, `.in` when you expected `.com`) are warnings.
2. **Look for misspellings.** `gpay-support.net`, `goog1e.com` (with a one instead of an L), `whatsapp-update.com` — none of these are the real company.
3. **A link inside a message is always worth suspicion.** Type the company’s name into Google yourself, or open the app you already have. Do not let the message choose the door.
4. **A padlock does not mean “genuine.”** The little lock and `https` mean the connection is encrypted. Scam sites have padlocks too. Encryption is not a badge of honesty.
5. **Do not trust a logo.** Anyone can copy a logo and paste it onto a webpage.

---

!!! danger "Never"
    - Tap “Install” from a link sent on WhatsApp, SMS, or email.
    - Log in to your bank on a site you reached through a message link.
    - Enter your OTP, PIN, or password on a page you are not sure you opened yourself.

---

## How to check a link is real

- Type the bank or service’s name in Google and open the result whose address you recognise. If you are unsure, do not log in there either — use the app you installed from Play Store or App Store.
- Call the official helpline printed on the back of your card and ask.
- Ask a family member who is comfortable with this, while you stay on your own phone. Do not hand them an OTP.

---

## If you already tapped and entered details

1. Change your password on the *real* website or app immediately — the one you open yourself, not the page from the message.
2. If any payment information, OTP, or PIN was entered, call your bank on the number on the card.
3. Report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or call **1930**.
