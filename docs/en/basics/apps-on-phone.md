# Apps on your phone

Every app you install is a small program living on your phone. If you allow it, it can use the camera, read your contacts, see your location, or look at files. Most apps are harmless. A few are built to watch you or empty an account.

You do not need to fear every install. You need a rule about *where* an app comes from, and a habit of saying no to permissions that do not match the job.

---

## Steps

1. **Install only from Play Store (Android) or App Store (iPhone).** Never from a link on WhatsApp, SMS, email, or a website that says “download the APK.” An **APK** is an Android install file. Sending one in a chat is a common way to skip the store’s checks.
2. **Read the developer name before you install.** The official BHIM app is by NPCI. Fake apps copy the icon and hope you do not look at the line underneath.
3. **When the app asks for permission, match it to the job.** A torch does not need your contacts. A calculator does not need your location. If it asks, deny it. You can still use the torch.
4. **Delete apps you do not use.** An unused app can still sit there, collect data, and pick up new permissions in an update you barely notice.
5. **Keep the phone and its apps updated.** Those updates are often quiet repairs for security holes. Waiting months is how old holes stay open.

---

!!! danger "Never install"
    - APK files from WhatsApp, Telegram, or any website.
    - Any app that promises “double your money,” “free recharge,” or “government cash.”
    - Remote access tools (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, Quick Support) because an unexpected caller asked you to. Those tools let another person see or control your screen. They belong in a planned session with a company’s IT team you already know — not on a personal phone during a surprise call.

---

## If you already installed something suspicious

1. Uninstall it. If you cannot, turn on airplane mode so it cannot talk to the internet.
2. Check recent bank and UPI transactions on a *different* device if you can, or after the app is gone.
3. Change passwords for email and banking from a phone or computer you trust.
4. If you used the app, or it asked for screen control, report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) and consider a factory reset after you back up photos. [After a mistake](../life/if-something-goes-wrong.md) walks through this more slowly.
