# OTP, UPI, and money

OTP (One-Time Password) and UPI PIN are the two keys to your money. Losing them is like handing over your wallet and walking away.

---

## Steps

1. **An OTP arrives on your phone — do not read it aloud and do not type it anywhere except the app that sent it to you.**
2. **Your UPI PIN is only for the UPI app's PIN box.** You never need to tell it to a person.
3. **Receiving money on UPI does not require you to enter your PIN.** If someone says "enter your PIN to receive ₹5,000," they are stealing money, not sending it.
4. **Check the UPI ID before you pay.** A name like "HDFC Bank Refund" means nothing — anyone can set that display name. Check the UPI ID (the part that ends in @okhdfc or similar).
5. **Set a daily UPI limit.** Most apps let you set a maximum daily transfer amount. Use it.

---

!!! danger "Never"
    - Share an OTP with anyone on a call, chat, or SMS.
    - Enter your UPI PIN to "receive" money.
    - Pay a "refund" that asks you to enter an amount you want to receive.

---

## If you already shared an OTP or PIN

Call your bank immediately on the number on the back of your card. Ask them to freeze your account. Then report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or call **1930**.
