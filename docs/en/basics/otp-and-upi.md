# OTP, UPI, and money on your phone

In India, a lot of money now moves from the phone in your hand. Two small secrets make that possible. If someone else gets them, they can spend as you.

An **OTP** is a one-time password: a short code, usually 4 to 8 digits, that a bank or app texts you. It means “the person holding this phone agrees to this one action.” It is not a conversation. It is a key.

**UPI** is a way to send money using apps such as GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, or BHIM. Your **UPI PIN** is the number you enter in *that app* to say yes to sending money. You never need to tell it to a human being.

Losing either is close to handing over your wallet and walking away.

---

## How to keep the money yours

1. **When an OTP arrives, do not read it aloud.** Do not type it into a page you opened from a message. Use it only inside the app or site you opened yourself, in the box that is waiting for that code.
2. **Your UPI PIN lives in one place:** the PIN box inside your UPI app. Nobody on a call, in a shop, or in a “support” chat needs to hear it.
3. **Receiving money does not use your PIN.** If someone says “enter your PIN to receive ₹5,000,” they are not sending you money. They are lining up a payment *from* you. Put the phone down.
4. **Read the UPI ID, not the pretty name.** Anyone can set a display name like “HDFC Bank Refund.” The real address is the bit that looks like `name@okhdfc` or `name@ybl`. If you do not recognise it, do not pay.
5. **Set a daily UPI limit.** In most apps you can cap how much can leave in a day. A low ceiling will not stop every trick, but it stops a drained account while you are still noticing.

---

!!! danger "Never"
    - Share an OTP with anyone on a call, chat, or SMS.
    - Enter your UPI PIN to “receive” money.
    - Pay a “refund” that asks you to type in the amount you want to get back.

---

## A story that happens every week

A caller says they are from the bank. They already know your name and the last four digits of your card — details that leak in small ways all the time. They say the account will be blocked unless you “verify” the OTP that is about to arrive. The OTP arrives. You read it out. In seconds, a payment you did not intend is complete.

The bank did not call. The person who knew a little about you used it to sound like the bank.

If that call happens: hang up. Turn the card over. Dial the number printed there.

---

## If you already shared an OTP or PIN

Call your bank immediately on the number on the back of your card. Ask them to freeze the account. Then report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or call **1930**. Do not stay on the line with the person who asked for the code. They will not help you undo it.
