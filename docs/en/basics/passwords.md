# Passwords

A password is the thing that stands between “this is my account” and “anyone who can guess this is me.” You do not need twenty clever passwords in your head. You need one good habit, starting with email.

Your email is the master key. Forgot-password links for banking, shopping, WhatsApp, and almost everything else go there. If someone opens your email, they can open the rest.

---

## Steps to a safer password

1. **Give email its own password.** Do not reuse it on Facebook, a shopping site, or a gaming app. When one of those is leaked — and leaks happen — a reused password is a spare key to your inbox.
2. **Make it a sentence, not a word.** `MyDogRajuHatesRain@7` is far stronger than `raju123` or `Raju@2024`. Length beats clever substitutions. Do not use your birthday, your phone number, or a child’s name. Those are the first things a stranger tries.
3. **Do not reuse the same password on important accounts.** Bank, email, and UPI should not share a password with each other or with anything casual.
4. **Change it the moment you think someone may have seen it.** A shoulder on a bus, a photo of a sticky note, a password typed into a site you no longer trust — treat it as already used.
5. **Do not leave passwords on paper next to the phone, or in a WhatsApp chat with yourself.** Those are the first places a thief or a curious relative looks.

---

!!! danger "Never"
    - Share a password over WhatsApp, SMS, or a call. A real company will not ask you to.
    - Use your date of birth, phone number, or child’s name as a password.
    - Save passwords in a chat with yourself on WhatsApp.

---

## If you already reused a password

Change email first. Then change it everywhere you used the same one, starting with banking apps. Yes, it is tedious. It is still shorter than recovering a drained account.

---

## A note on password managers

A **password manager** is an app — Google’s built-in one on many Android phones and in Chrome is a common example — that remembers different passwords for you, behind the lock on your phone.

That is safer than using `Raju@123` everywhere. You still protect the manager with your phone lock (PIN, pattern, fingerprint). You are not “bad with technology” if you use one. You are refusing to reuse keys.
