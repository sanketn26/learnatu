# WhatsApp

For most of India, WhatsApp is the front door: family, work groups, school notices, payments talk, rumours, jokes. It is also the most common place a scam tries to look like a person you already trust.

You do not have to leave WhatsApp. You have to treat a message there as *a message*, not as proof.

---

## Habits that close the usual doors

1. **Turn on two-step verification.** This is a second PIN, inside WhatsApp, so someone who grabs a SIM card cannot move your chats to their phone. Path: **Settings → Account → Two-step verification.** Choose a 6-digit PIN you will remember, and do not share it.
2. **Decide who can see your profile photo and status.** **Settings → Privacy → Profile photo → My contacts.** A stranger who has your number does not need your face.
3. **Do not add unknown numbers to your contacts just to be polite.** Once they are a contact, more of your profile may become visible to them.
4. **A forwarded message is not news.** Before you pass it on, ask whether you can find the same claim on a news site you already trust. If you cannot, you can still tell one person you trust. You do not have to broadcast it.
5. **Unknown international numbers are often not worth answering.** +92, +1, +44 — if you were not expecting the call, let it go. A real bank or relative who needs you will find another way.

---

!!! danger "Never"
    - Share an OTP over WhatsApp with anyone.
    - Tap “Install” or “Download” on a link that arrived in a WhatsApp message.
    - Send a photo of Aadhaar, PAN, or a bank document over WhatsApp — even to someone you know — unless you are sure they need it and there is no safer channel.

---

## Privacy settings worth using

- **Profile photo:** My contacts
- **Last seen:** My contacts, or Nobody
- **Status:** My contacts
- **Groups:** My contacts (this stops strangers adding you to random groups)
- **Two-step verification:** On
- **Read receipts:** Your choice. Leaving them on helps you see that a message arrived. It is not a safety feature.

---

## If you already tapped a bad link

1. Do not type anything on the page that opened — no password, no OTP, no PIN.
2. Close the tab.
3. If you *did* enter something, change that password in the official app immediately, and call the bank if money was involved.
4. Clearing the browser’s stored data can help (Settings → Apps → your browser → Clear data), but changing the password matters more.
