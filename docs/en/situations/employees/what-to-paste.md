# What you may paste into ChatGPT

You want the email to sound more professional. Or you want a messy paragraph turned into bullet points. ChatGPT is a chatbot: you type or paste, and it writes a reply that sounds like a colleague. Gemini is Google’s version of the same idea. Both are useful for *how something is said*. They are a poor place to store *who it is about*.

**Who this is for:** Employees who use personal or free-tier AI accounts for work tasks.

**Risk level:** Depends on what you paste. See the table below.

---

## The risk table

| What you want to paste | Risk level | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A question about something publicly known (e.g. "How do I write a formal email?") | 🟢 Safe | Fine to use |
| Your own draft email (no names, no clients) | 🟢 Safe | Fine to use |
| A customer's name and phone number | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| Payroll figures or salary information | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| A legal contract (draft or signed) | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| A medical file or patient record | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| Unpublished source code | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| API keys or credentials | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |
| A meeting transcript that includes clients | 🔴 Never upload | Do not paste |

An **API key** is a secret password that lets one program talk to another. If it leaks, someone else can use that service as you. **Credentials** here means logins, passwords, and similar keys.

---

!!! danger "Never upload"
    Customer data, payroll, legal contracts, medical files, source code, API keys, and meeting transcripts with clients must never go into a personal or free AI account. This could violate your employment contract, Indian data protection rules, and your clients' trust.

---

## A safe prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool. For tasks that are safe:

> "Help me rewrite this email to sound more professional. Here is my draft: [paste your own text with no names or confidential details]."

---

## How to verify the answer

1. Read the AI's output and ask: does it contain made-up names, facts, or figures? Remove any that are not true.
2. Ask a colleague or manager to review before sending anything based on AI output.
3. Check your company's policy on AI tools — some employers have specific guidance or approved tools.

---

## Checklist

Before the text goes into the box, walk through this once:

- [ ] Does my paste contain any customer name, phone, or address? If yes, do not paste.
- [ ] Does my paste contain any salary, payment, or financial data? If yes, do not paste.
- [ ] Does my paste contain an unpublished contract or internal document? If yes, do not paste.
- [ ] Have I removed all API keys and passwords before pasting code?
- [ ] Did I check my company's AI usage policy?

---

## Privacy settings to use

- **ChatGPT:** Settings → Data controls → turn off "Improve the model for everyone." When that setting is on, the company may use what you type to train — teach — its software. Work text does not belong in that stream.
- **Google Gemini:** Gemini Apps Activity → Pause. That stops new conversations being saved into your Google activity history.
- **Work account vs. personal:** If your company provides a Microsoft Copilot or Google Workspace AI, prefer that over a personal account. Those sit inside the company’s agreement; a free personal login does not.
- **Browser:** Do not let your browser auto-fill personal passwords into AI tool login pages.
- **Phone:** Lock your screen when stepping away from your desk, so a chat with work text in it is not open to whoever walks past.

## A realistic failure

Vikram was preparing a proposal and wanted to clean up the language. He pasted an entire contract draft — the client’s name, the project value, the internal pricing — into his personal ChatGPT account. The free version, at the time, used conversations to train the software.

The rewrite came back polished. He used it. Months later, during a client audit, his manager learned that confidential terms had been submitted to a third-party server. Vikram had to explain a paste he had thought of as a writing shortcut.

If he had paused and taken out the client name and the rupee figures, he could still have asked for help with the tone. The sentences were the useful part. The names and numbers were the part that was not his to give away.
