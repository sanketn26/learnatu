# Employees

The email has to go out before lunch. The draft is messy. A colleague mentioned ChatGPT — a chatbot: you paste some text, it rewrites it in a calmer tone. You highlight a paragraph from the client file, copy, and the paste box is waiting. That is the moment this section is about.

You already use apps all day at work — email, chat, document editors, and now tools such as ChatGPT or Gemini (Google’s similar chatbot). The question that matters most is not “is AI allowed?” It is: what is safe to paste in, and what is not.

## Two habits that keep work data at work

Some information is like a key. Customer names, phone numbers, payroll figures, contracts, and unpublished source code (the text of a program the company has not released) do not belong in a personal chatbot account. Once they are in that box, you cannot pull them back. Your company’s data is not yours to share.

The second habit is a pause. Before you copy from a work document and paste into any online tool, ask: does my employer know I am doing this? Would they be comfortable if the client saw it happen? If you are not sure, take the names and numbers out first — or do not paste it.
