# Customer data

You keep a spreadsheet because memory does not scale: who ordered, which medicine, which flat. A delivery partner asks for the link. A chatbot could “sort it by city” if you paste the columns in. The list feels like yours. It is made of other people’s lives.

**Who this is for:** Small business owners who collect customer names, phone numbers, addresses, or payment details.

**Risk level:**

!!! danger "Never upload"
    Customer names, phone numbers, and order details must not be uploaded to personal AI accounts, WhatsApp groups, or any platform outside your own devices without clear reason and customer consent.

---

## What could go wrong

- You paste a customer list into ChatGPT to "sort it" — that data is now on a third-party server. **ChatGPT** is a chatbot: you paste text, it replies. The paste does not stay only on your phone.
- You share a list of customer phone numbers in a group chat — one member uses it for unsolicited marketing.
- Your phone is stolen with an unprotected spreadsheet of 500 customer orders.
- A delivery app you used retained customer addresses and sold them to a competitor.
- India now has a law called the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, or **DPDPA**. In plain language: if you collect a customer's name, phone number, or order history, you are responsible for keeping it safe. A leak can mean complaints, fines, or the quieter damage of customers who stop trusting you.

---

## A safe prompt

A **prompt** is the message you type into the tool. Do not use AI here with real data. For learning, use fake data:

> "Here is a sample customer list with fake names: [list]. Help me sort it by city."

When you are done learning the format, apply it yourself to your real data locally.

---

## How to verify you are handling data safely

1. Ask: is this customer data on my own device, or on someone else's server?
2. If you are using a cloud tool (Google Sheets, etc.), check who has access to the file.
3. If a vendor asks for your customer list, ask why they need it and what they will do with it.

---

## Checklist

Before a list leaves your phone or a sheet is shared, walk through this once:

- [ ] Is my customer spreadsheet password-protected or in a locked app?
- [ ] Have I avoided pasting real customer names or numbers into any AI tool?
- [ ] Do I know where all copies of my customer list are?
- [ ] Have I deleted customer data I no longer need?
- [ ] Does my business have a simple privacy notice for customers (even one sentence on your invoice)?

---

## Privacy settings to use

- **Google Sheets:** Share with specific people only — not "anyone with the link." “Anyone with the link” means a forward is enough; the recipient does not even need a Google account.
- **WhatsApp Business:** Do not store customer lists in broadcast groups that include non-staff members.
- **Phone:** Enable screen lock and encrypt your device (Android: Settings → Security → Encryption). Encryption scrambles the files so a stolen phone without your PIN is much harder to read.
- **Email:** Use a business email address, not a personal Gmail, for customer communications.
- **Backup:** Store customer data backups on a physical drive you own, not only in a free cloud account.

## A realistic failure

Deepak runs a small pharmacy. He kept regular customers’ names, addresses, and the medicines they ordered in a Google Sheet, and he shared it with a “anyone with the link” address because that was faster than adding people one by one. He sent the link to a delivery partner.

That partner forwarded it. A third person, who ran a competing pharmacy, opened it. Deepak’s customers started getting calls from a service they had never contacted. The medicines column made the list more valuable, and more harmful, than a phone book.

If he had set the sheet to “Specific people” instead of “Anyone with the link,” the data would have stayed inside his team. Speed of sharing was the leak.
