# Seniors

The phone rings. The name on the screen looks like a bank. The voice says there is a problem with the account, and that you must share a code that will arrive in a moment, or the account will be blocked today. Your heart rate goes up. That rush is the point of the call.

If you are reading this for an elderly parent or relative, this section is for you. If you are a senior reading it yourself — welcome. These pages explain the scams that target seniors most often, and how to recognise them before any money moves.

## Two habits that close the usual traps

If someone is hurrying you — pay now, share the code now, do not tell your family — treat that hurry as a warning. A real bank, police officer, or government official will never call you and demand immediate payment or an **OTP**. An OTP is a one-time password: a short code, usually 4 to 8 digits, that a bank texts you to prove you have the phone in your hand. Urgency is the scam’s main tool.

The other habit: use the official app and the official number only. The bank’s number is on the back of your card, or on the bank’s own website that *you* type. The government’s number is on a site ending in `.gov.in`. Trust those. Do not trust a number that called you, however official the voice sounded.
