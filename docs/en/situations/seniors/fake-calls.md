# Fake calls and messages

**Who this is for:** Seniors and their families who want to recognise calls and messages from fraudsters pretending to be from banks, police, or government departments.

**Risk level:**

!!! danger "Never"
    Never share an OTP, PIN, account number, or Aadhaar details with anyone who called you. A real bank or government officer will never ask for these over the phone.

---

## What could go wrong

- A caller says "I am from the bank, your account will be blocked — share your OTP to save it." The OTP transfers money out of your account.
- A message says "You have won a lottery — pay ₹500 to claim your ₹50,000." There is no lottery.
- A "policeman" calls and says you are in legal trouble. He asks for money via UPI to "close the case quietly."
- A video call shows a person in police uniform — it could be a costume bought online.
- A message with a government logo says your Aadhaar is linked to a crime. It is fake.

---

## A safe prompt

Do not use AI here. When in doubt, end the call.

**Human steps instead:**

1. End the call without agreeing to anything.
2. Call back the bank using the number on the back of your card, or call a trusted family member.
3. Do not call back the number that called you — it may be a scammer's number.

---

## How to verify a call is real

1. Hang up. Call the official number (bank card, gov.in website). Ask if they tried to reach you.
2. Ask a family member to check whether the claim is real before you do anything.
3. A real government notice comes by post or registered email — not a WhatsApp message.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Did you end the call without sharing any number or code?
- [ ] Did you call the official number to verify?
- [ ] Did you tell a family member about the call?
- [ ] Did you not press any number or "confirm" anything on a call you did not initiate?
- [ ] If money was already sent, did you call your bank immediately?

---

## Privacy settings to use

- **WhatsApp:** Settings → Privacy → Last seen → Nobody (stops strangers from knowing when you are active).
- **Phone calls:** Enable "caller ID and spam" in your phone's settings to flag known scam numbers.
- **SMS:** Report spam SMS to TRAI by forwarding to 1909.
- **Do Not Disturb:** Register at [dndregister.trai.gov.in](https://dndregister.trai.gov.in) to reduce unsolicited calls.
- **Bank app:** Enable transaction alerts for every debit so you know immediately if money leaves.

---

## A realistic failure

Sixty-eight-year-old Ramesh received a call from someone claiming to be from "SBI Fraud Prevention." The man knew Ramesh's full name and the last four digits of his card — details available from a previous small data leak. He said Ramesh's account was flagged and he needed to "verify" himself by sharing the OTP that would arrive on his phone. Ramesh, frightened, shared it. ₹42,000 left his account in seconds. If Ramesh had ended the call and called the SBI helpline (1800 11 2211, free), he would have confirmed in two minutes that no one from SBI had called him.
