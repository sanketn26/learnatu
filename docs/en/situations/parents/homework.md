# Homework help

**Who this is for:** Parents whose children ask to use Google, ChatGPT, or Gemini for school assignments.

**Risk level:**

!!! warning "Caution"
    AI tools can help, but they invent facts. Every answer needs a check before it goes into a submission.

---

## What could go wrong

- The AI gives a plausible-sounding but completely wrong historical fact, and the child submits it as true.
- The child copies an AI paragraph word for word, which may count as plagiarism at their school.
- The child uploads a photo of a test paper or homework sheet, and that question bank is now in a company's training data.
- An AI chatbot in "conversation mode" asks personal questions the child answers freely.
- The child learns to accept the first answer without questioning it — a habit that harms critical thinking.

---

## A safe prompt

Ask the AI to explain, not to answer directly:

> "Explain how photosynthesis works in simple words. Do not make up any details. If you are not certain about something, say so."

Then have the child verify the explanation against their textbook or a trusted site like [ncert.nic.in](https://ncert.nic.in).

---

## How to verify the answer

1. Open the child's textbook and find the same topic. Do the key facts match?
2. Search the topic on a government or school board website (ncert.nic.in, byjus.com, or the school's own resources).
3. Ask the class teacher if the answer looks different from what was taught.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Did the child read and understand the AI answer, not just copy it?
- [ ] Did they check one fact against the textbook?
- [ ] Did they avoid uploading the question paper or test sheet?
- [ ] Does the submitted work sound like the child wrote it?
- [ ] Did the child mark which parts used AI (if their school requires it)?

---

## Privacy settings to use

- **ChatGPT:** Settings → Data controls → turn off "Improve the model for everyone" so chats are not used for training.
- **Google Gemini:** Activity controls → turn off Gemini Apps Activity to stop saving conversations.
- **Browser:** Use a browser that doesn't auto-fill passwords into AI chatbots.
- **School account:** Use a school-provided account if available — it has extra protections.
- **Age check:** Most AI tools require users to be 13+. Supervise children under 13.

---

## A realistic failure

Priya's daughter Meera, 11, had a history project due the next morning. She asked ChatGPT "When did the Mughal Empire end?" and got a confident answer with a specific year. She wrote it in her project. Her teacher marked it wrong — the AI had confused two different events. If Meera had paused and checked the same question in her Class 7 history textbook, she would have found the correct answer in two minutes. The pause would have saved the marks.
