# Deepfakes and harmful content

**Who this is for:** Parents who have found, or suspect, fabricated images or videos involving their child or someone they know.

**Risk level:**

!!! danger "Never upload"
    Do not upload images of the affected person to any AI tool or website to "check" whether they are fake. Report the content directly using the steps below.

---

## What could go wrong

- A photo taken from a child's social media profile is used to create a fabricated image.
- A video call recording is manipulated to show someone saying things they never said.
- A fabricated image is shared in a school WhatsApp group and spreads before anyone can stop it.
- The child or family member feels unable to tell a parent because they are ashamed, even though they did nothing wrong.

---

## A safe prompt

Do not use AI here. AI tools cannot reliably detect deepfakes, and uploading the image creates a new privacy risk.

**Human steps instead:**

1. Screenshot the post URL (not the image itself) as evidence.
2. Report the content to the platform (WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube) using the Report button.
3. Call the school if it involves classmates.
4. Contact the police cybercrime helpline: **1930** or [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in).

---

## How to verify the content is fabricated

1. Look at the face carefully — unnatural blinking, mismatched lighting, or blurry edges around the face are common signs.
2. Call the person in the video directly and ask if they recorded it.
3. Check whether the video or image appeared first on a platform known for fake content.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Did you take a screenshot of the URL (not the image) as evidence?
- [ ] Did you report the post to the platform directly?
- [ ] Did you tell the child it is not their fault and they did the right thing by telling you?
- [ ] Did you contact the school if classmates are involved?
- [ ] Have you filed a report at cybercrime.gov.in?

---

## Privacy settings to use

- **Instagram:** Settings → Privacy → Private account (restricts who can see photos).
- **Facebook:** Profile → Edit privacy → Past posts → Friends only.
- **WhatsApp:** Settings → Privacy → Profile photo → My contacts.
- **Google:** Search your child's name periodically and request removal of unwanted results via Google's removal tool.
- **School group chats:** Ask the admin to restrict who can send messages.

---

## A realistic failure

Fourteen-year-old Arun's class photo was taken from his school's public Facebook page. A fake account used it to create a fabricated video and posted it in his school's WhatsApp group. Within an hour, 40 students had seen it. His mother found out when another parent called. She panicked and forwarded the video to her family to "warn them" — which spread it further. The right pause would have been: screenshot the URL, report it to WhatsApp, call the school, and not forward the content at all.
