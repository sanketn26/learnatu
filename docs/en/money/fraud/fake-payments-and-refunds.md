# Fake payments and refund tricks

A buyer on WhatsApp wants the used fridge. They send a screenshot: green tick, ₹12,000, “payment successful.” They are in a hurry for the address. Or a caller says they paid too much and you must refund the extra — scan this QR, enter your PIN, “it will reverse.”

A screenshot, an SMS, an email, or a caller’s screen can be fabricated. Only your own verified account can confirm that money arrived.

A **QR code** is that square patch of dots. Scanning it often prepares a payment *from* you, not a credit *to* you. Your **UPI PIN** is the number you enter in your payment app to say yes to sending money. Receiving money does not use that PIN. If someone says “enter your PIN to receive ₹5,000,” they are not sending you money. They are lining up a payment from you.

## Common versions

- A buyer shows a fake successful-payment screen
- Someone claims to have paid too much and requests a refund
- A caller sends a QR code to “receive” or “reverse” money
- A small genuine payment is used to gain trust before a larger request
- A refund requires a PIN, OTP, screen share, or remote-access app

An **OTP** is a one-time password: a short code a bank or app texts you for one action. A remote-access app lets someone else see or control your phone. Neither belongs in a “refund.”

Open your bank or payment app yourself and check the completed transaction and available balance. Do not use a link in the message. Look for a credit that has actually settled — money you can spend — not a “processing” line and not a picture of someone else’s screen.

Suresh listed a fridge on a local group. The buyer paid ₹1 from a different name “to confirm the number,” then sent a screenshot of ₹18,000 and asked him to refund ₹6,000 because they had “tapped twice.” Suresh opened PhonePe himself. The ₹1 was there. The ₹18,000 was not. He did not refund. He stopped replying.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Trust a payment screenshot, SMS, or caller's screen as proof money arrived.
    - Scan a QR code or share a PIN, OTP, or screen access to "receive" or "reverse" a payment.
    - Refund an "extra payment" before your own bank or payment app confirms cleared money was received.

If a "refund" request feels rushed, that pressure is itself a warning sign — a genuine overpayment can wait for you to verify it properly. A real buyer who paid extra can wait while you check. A fake one cannot, because the screenshot is the whole trick.

## If you already sent a “refund”

Hang up or stop the chat. Open your own bank or UPI app and see what actually left. Call the bank on the number on the back of the card, or through the official app. In India, report promptly through **1930** and [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in). Do not send a second payment to “unlock” or reverse the first one. That request is the next trick, not the way back.
