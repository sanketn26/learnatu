# How financial fraud creates pressure

Financial fraud is usually a sequence, not one clever message. The criminal first gains attention, creates trust or fear, isolates the person, and then demands an action.

## Common pressure tools

- **Urgency:** act now or lose the opportunity
- **Authority:** bank, police, employer, regulator, or government
- **Emotion:** fear, greed, affection, embarrassment, or helpfulness
- **Secrecy:** do not tell family, bank staff, or colleagues
- **Small compliance:** click, scan, install, share screen, or make a test payment
- **Sunk cost:** pay again to recover earlier money

!!! danger "Do not"
    - Act immediately just because a message or caller creates urgency or fear.
    - Keep the request secret from family, bank staff, or colleagues.
    - Trust a caller ID, logo, document, or voice as proof of identity.

The safest interruption is simple: end the conversation and verify through a channel you already trust.

> A familiar voice, caller ID, logo, document, or video is evidence to check—not proof.
