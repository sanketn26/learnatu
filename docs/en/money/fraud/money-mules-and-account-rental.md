# Never rent or lend your bank account

A neighbour, or a cousin’s friend, offers work that sounds like nothing: receive money in your account, keep a cut, forward the rest. Sometimes they want a new account opened in your name, or a SIM in your name, or help “converting” funds. They may call it a part-time job, company collections, or account rental.

What they are asking is for you to become a **money mule**: a person whose account, SIM, or identity is used to move money that came from fraud. When the bank and the police follow the trail, the trail ends at *your* KYC — **KYC** is the identity check (know your customer) that tied the account to your name, photograph, and Aadhaar or PAN. The stranger who “only needed the account” is no longer on the screen.

## Warning phrases

- “Keep a commission and send the rest”
- “We only need your account for company payments”
- “Receive customer money while working from home”
- “Open an account or wallet for us”
- “Convert these funds to cryptocurrency”

**Cryptocurrency** is a digital token whose value can swing or vanish, traded outside ordinary bank rails. Being asked to turn rupees from a stranger into crypto, or the other way around, is a common way stolen money is moved. It does not become legal because someone called it a “task.”

Lakshmi was offered ₹5,000 a month to “collect vendor payments” in her savings account. The first credit was ₹40,000 from a name she did not know, with a message: keep 2,000, UPI the rest today. She did not. She called her bank on the number in the official app and told them unexpected money had arrived with instructions to forward it. That call is how you step off the trail, not a chat with the sender.

Do not share account access, cards, SIMs, OTPs, PINs, identity documents, or signed blank forms. An **OTP** is a one-time password: a short code that means you agree to this one action. Your **UPI PIN** says yes to sending money. Neither is a tool you lend. Do not move suspicious money onward, even for someone you know.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Let someone else receive or move money through your bank account, wallet, or SIM.
    - Share your account, card, SIM, OTP, or PIN with a stranger or acquaintance for "commission."
    - Convert or forward suspicious funds, even for someone you know.

If unexpected money arrives with instructions to forward it, contact your bank through an official channel and preserve the messages. Do not negotiate privately with the sender. Do not return the money to a new UPI ID they dictate. Returning it “to be helpful” can still be you moving stolen funds.

## If this already happened

Stop forwarding. Do not delete the chat. Contact your bank through the official app or the number on the card, tell them the account may have been used by someone else, and ask what they need you to do. In India, report through **1930** and [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in). The earlier the bank hears it from you, the clearer it is that you are not trying to hide the trail.
