# Beware of recovery scams

The first fraud is over. Money has left. You are still replaying the call. Then a new number rings. They know the amount. They know the UPI ID. They say they are from cybercrime, or a bank’s “recovery cell,” or a lawyer, or even a hacker who can reverse the payment — if you pay an investigation fee today, and if you do not tell anyone about the special process.

They know details of the first fraud because they are often the same people, or people who bought your number from the first crew. After a loss, criminals may contact you again pretending to be investigators, lawyers, hackers, bank staff, or recovery agents. They use those details to sound credible.

## They may request

- An investigation, tax, release, or processing fee
- Remote access to your phone
- Account login, OTP, PIN, or recovery code
- Another transfer to trace or unlock the first payment
- Secrecy about their “special process”

A **processing fee** in this setting is not a bank charge. It is a second theft wearing a form’s name. An **OTP** is a one-time password: a short code that means you agree to this one action. A **PIN** (including a UPI PIN) says yes to sending money. A recovery code is the backup key to an email or app. Remote access means they can see or control the phone. None of these is how money is returned in India.

Anjali had already lost ₹18,000 to a fake job task. Two days later a man sent her a PDF with her own transaction ID on it and asked for ₹4,500 as a “release fee” so the police portal could refund her. The transaction ID was real — she had typed it into a group herself. She hung up, called **1930**, and reported at the official site. Nobody honest needs a fee from the victim to start that process.

Do not send more money. Report through your bank and official authorities using contact information you find independently — the number on the back of the card, the bank’s own app, **1930**, [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in). Do not use a number or link the “agent” sent you.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Pay a fee to a "recovery agent" who contacts you after a fraud.
    - Give remote access to your phone or share your login, OTP, PIN, or recovery code with anyone claiming to trace or unlock lost money.
    - Send another transfer to "recover" or "unlock" a previous payment.

In India, report financial cybercrime promptly through **1930** and [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in). No private person can guarantee recovery. A bank or the official cybercrime process may or may not get money back; that depends on speed, the type of payment, and facts they will ask you for. A stranger who guarantees it, for a fee, is selling the second loss.

## If they are on the line now

Hang up. Do not argue about their badge. Call your bank on a number you already have, then **1930**. Save the new number, the messages, and the PDFs as they are. Tell one person in the house, so the next “confidential” call is not yours alone.
