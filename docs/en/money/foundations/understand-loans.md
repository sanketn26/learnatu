# Understand a loan before accepting it

The ad shows a monthly payment that looks small. ₹4,800 a month. You can picture it leaving after salary. What the ad does not show in the same large type is how many months, what you actually receive, and what else is taken out before the money hits your account.

That monthly payment is an **EMI** — an equated monthly instalment, the amount the lender wants on a fixed date. Affordable-looking instalments can hide a large total cost. Compare the complete obligation, not only the EMI.

## Ask for these facts in writing

Do not accept a verbal “don’t worry, it is standard.” Ask the lender, on paper or in the official app, for:

- **Amount you actually receive.** After every deduction, not the headline loan amount.
- **Interest rate and how it is calculated.** Interest is the extra you pay for using the lender’s money. Whether it is charged on the shrinking balance or in some other way changes the total.
- **Every processing, insurance, platform, and late fee.** A **processing fee** is money the lender keeps for opening the loan. It is often cut from the amount you receive, so you borrow ₹50,000 and hold less than that.
- **EMI amount and number of payments.** Twelve payments of ₹4,800 is not the same product as twenty-four payments of ₹4,800.
- **Total amount repaid.** All EMIs added together, including fees you will pay along the way.
- **Consequences of missed or early payments.** What a late fee looks like, what happens to your credit record, whether you can close the loan early and at what cost.
- **Whether security or a guarantor is involved.** Security is something they can claim if you do not pay — gold, a vehicle, a house. A guarantor is a person who becomes responsible if you do not.

If a number is missing, the comparison is not finished. Wait.

## A realistic example

A loan app offers ₹50,000 with a monthly instalment of ₹4,800 for 12 months. That looks like ₹57,600 total — but the app also lists a ₹2,500 processing fee and a mandatory ₹1,800 insurance add-on taken out of the amount you receive. Your real amount received is ₹45,700, while your real total repaid is ₹57,600. The true cost is higher than the EMI alone suggests. Always calculate cost against what you actually receive, not the amount advertised.

AI can explain general loan terms, but it may calculate incorrectly or miss contract conditions. Verify calculations and terms against the lender's official written disclosure. For Indian financial education and alerts, consult the [Reserve Bank of India](https://www.rbi.org.in/). The RBI is India’s central bank and banking regulator. A page like this can show you what to compare. It cannot tell you whether *this* loan is right for your household.

An **OTP** is a one-time password: a short code, usually 4 to 8 digits, that a bank or app texts you. It means the person holding this phone agrees to this one action. A lending app that wants your contacts, or a stranger who wants that OTP to “release” the loan, is not doing paperwork. They are lining up control of your phone or your account.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Pay an advance fee to "release" or "unlock" a loan.
    - Share an OTP or let an app access your contacts without a clear, legitimate reason.
    - Install a lending app from a link in a message rather than an official app store.

## Before you tap accept

Write the seven facts on paper. If the app or the agent cannot put them in writing, treat that as the answer. If you already paid an “unlock” fee to a stranger, stop sending more. Contact your bank through the official app or the number on the back of your card, and report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in) or **1930**.
