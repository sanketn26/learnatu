# Build an emergency fund

The scooter will not start on a work morning. A parent needs a hospital deposit. A week of rain, or a week of no orders, and the income that usually arrives does not. None of these is a luxury. None of them waits for the next salary date.

An **emergency fund** is money kept within reach for unexpected essential costs: urgent travel, a repair, a stretch of lost work, medical needs. It is not meant to grow quickly. It is meant to still be there on a bad day. That is a different job from investing.

## Start without an intimidating target

Advice on the internet loves a round number of months. Your first job is smaller: a first pile you can actually build without skipping food or taking a new loan to look prepared.

1. **Choose a small first milestone.** One week of groceries. A typical hospital auto fare plus a night’s deposit. A number that would let you breathe once. Why: a distant target that feels impossible is how people never begin.

2. **Keep it separate from everyday spending when practical.** A different bank account, a different UPI handle, or a labelled envelope if that is what you trust. Why: money that sits in the same balance as “pay the electrician” gets spent as “pay the electrician.”

3. **Add a manageable amount after income arrives.** The day the salary or the week’s takings land, move the amount before the month talks you out of it. Why: what is left on the 28th is rarely what you meant to save on the 1st.

4. **Refill it after using it.** The fund did its job. The next job is to put it back, in the same small steps, when life is stable again. Why: an empty emergency fund is only a story you used to have.

5. **Increase the target gradually as your situation allows.** After the first milestone holds, aim a little higher. Why: needs change. Someone with variable income, dependants, health costs, or unstable work may need a different reserve from someone with a predictable salary. Avoid borrowing, or skipping essential needs, merely to reach a generic target you saw online.

Yusuf delivers food on a scooter. He started with ₹2,000 in a savings account he does not open for groceries. A puncture and a lost day of work used most of it. He treated that as the fund working, not as failure, and began putting ₹200 back each week that he earned. The number is his. The method is the point.

Keep emergency money in a place you understand, can reach when you need it, and have checked yourself — the real bank or post office, opened through an official app or a branch, not a stranger’s “scheme.” “Guaranteed high-return emergency funds” are a contradiction. If it promises both instant safety and a high return, it is not an emergency fund. It is a sales pitch.

This is not personal advice for your household. It is a way to start. If you already have debt with high interest, or a health cost that cannot wait, a qualified person or your bank’s official channel can help you weigh the next rupee. The fund’s purpose stays the same: a shock should not have to become a second loan.

## If you already used the money

That is what it was for. Do not replace it with a “quick” loan app if you can refill it from income. Write the new, smaller balance. Choose the next small milestone from there.
