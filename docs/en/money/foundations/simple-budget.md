# Make a simple budget

The salary is in. Someone in the house already has a plan for it — a repair, a festival purchase, a loan instalment — and someone else has a different plan. By evening the argument is not really about the purchase. It is about the fact that the money was never given jobs in advance.

A **budget** is that set of jobs. It is a plan for decisions, not a punishment and not a personality test. Start with the money you actually have and the responsibilities you actually carry, not with a template meant for a different household.

## A practical order

Give the month a sequence, so the loudest want does not go first.

1. **Cover essential needs and minimum required payments.** Food, housing, utilities, transport, medicine, school, and the smallest payment each loan or card demands this month. Why: everything else is optional until these are safe. An unpaid minimum payment also grows — **interest** is the extra the lender charges because the debt is still there.

2. **Set aside something for irregular expenses.** Festivals, annual fees, repairs, insurance. Even a small amount labelled “October school shoes” is more useful than hoping October will be kind. Why: a cost you can see coming should not have to borrow.

3. **Build a small emergency reserve.** Money you can reach if income pauses or something essential breaks. Why: without this, the next shock becomes a new loan. How to grow it is a separate page; the budget’s job is to give it a line.

4. **Decide what remains for flexible spending and longer-term goals.** The meal out, the new phone, the savings for a course or a house deposit. Why: leftover money without a job gets spent by accident. Named leftover money can wait.

5. **Review what actually happened at the end of the month.** Compare the plan with the bank app and the cash that left the house. Why: a budget that is never compared with real life becomes fiction, and fiction is what people abandon.

When income changes from month to month, write a **minimum month** plan: the version that still covers essentials if work is thin. Decide in advance how extra income in a better month will be divided — some to the reserve, some to irregular costs, some to a goal — so a good week does not disappear as “we managed, so we spent.”

No budgeting formula fits every household. The useful budget is the one you can understand, adjust, and follow.

## Safe AI prompt

> Create a blank monthly budget template for a household with variable income. Use categories only. Do not ask for account numbers, names, addresses, or exact bank records.

Use the result as empty headings. Put your own numbers in yourself, on paper or in a note that stays on your phone.

## Do this for the month already in progress

Pick one category that always surprises you — vegetables, fuel, mobile recharges, school. Write last month’s rough total next to it. That single number is already a budget. You can add the rest of the headings when you have them.
