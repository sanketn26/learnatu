# Save, invest, or repay debt?

Salary week. After rent and groceries, a little is left. A WhatsApp group is talking about a “sure” return. The credit-card bill, or a loan app EMI — the monthly instalment — is also waiting. Three different people will tell you three different first moves. They cannot all be right for this money, this month.

These choices serve different purposes. The right order depends on urgency, cost, time, and risk — not on what is popular online.

**Saving** usually means putting money where you can reach it and where the number is not meant to swing: a bank account, a deposit you understand. **Investing** means accepting that the value can go down as well as up, in the hope of a longer-term goal. **Repaying debt** means reducing a cost you already owe. **Interest** on a loan or card is the extra you pay because the debt is still there; interest on savings is the extra the bank pays you. They are not the same rate, and they are not a reason to treat every rupee as an investment.

## Questions to ask

Sit with the actual leftover amount, or the actual extra payment you could make, and answer these on paper:

- **Will I need this money soon?** School fees in three months, a security deposit, next quarter’s insurance premium — the regular amount an insurance policy costs — are not long-term money.
- **Could I tolerate its value falling?** If a drop would mean you cannot pay rent or medicine, this money is not for products whose price moves.
- **What interest and fees am I paying on debt?** A card or a loan app that charges far more than you could earn elsewhere is a cost that continues every month you wait.
- **Do I have accessible money for emergencies?** If the next hospital deposit or lost week of work would force a new loan, that gap may need filling before a long-term product.
- **Do I understand how this product works and how I can exit?** If you cannot say when you get the money back, what you can lose, and what fee you pay to leave, you are not ready to put money in.
- **Is the provider properly regulated for what it offers?** A glossy app and a “SEBI registered” image in a PDF are not the register. **SEBI** is the Securities and Exchange Board of India, the regulator for securities markets and many investment advisers. Check on official sites, not on the seller’s link.

Saving usually prioritises access and stability. Investing accepts uncertainty for a longer-term goal. Repaying expensive debt can reduce a known cost. None of these automatically wins in every situation. A household with a high-interest loan and no emergency money is not in the same place as a household with a cheap home loan and a stable reserve. The questions above are how you see the difference.

Never invest because an AI tool, influencer, messaging group, or stranger promises a particular return. Use [SEBI Investor](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/) for Indian investor education and verification resources.

This is not personal advice about *your* leftover salary. It is a way to stop a stranger’s screenshot from making the decision. For a product that would move a large amount, or for tax questions, use official resources and a person who is qualified to advise you.

## Do this with the next leftover amount

Write the six questions. Answer them for *this* money, not for money in general. If two answers collide — you need the money soon *and* a group is promising a high return — the “soon” answer wins. The group can wait. Your rent cannot.
