# Verify a financial product or adviser

The person has a laptop, a certificate in a plastic sleeve, and a story about a product that “SEBI has approved.” The website looks like a bank. The testimonials have full names and photos. They would like your PAN, a cancelled cheque, and a first payment today, because the window closes this week.

Professional-looking websites, certificates, apps, and testimonials can be copied. Verify independently before sharing documents or money.

**SEBI** is the Securities and Exchange Board of India (investments and many advisers). **RBI** is the Reserve Bank of India (banks and many lending products). **IRDAI** is the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (insurance). **PFRDA** is the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (pensions such as NPS). Each keeps official lists. A seller’s PDF is not one of those lists.

## Verification steps

1. **Identify which regulator or authority should cover the product or provider.** A savings account is not an insurance policy. A pension is not a trading app. Why: fraud often borrows the name of one regulator to sell something that regulator does not oversee.

2. **Search that authority's official register yourself.** Type the official address yourself, or start from a page you already trust. Why: a “register” link in the seller’s WhatsApp is still the seller’s website, wearing a government logo.

3. **Confirm the exact legal name, registration details, website, phone number, and payment account.** Match spellings, registration numbers, and the account you are asked to pay into. Why: one extra letter in a company name, or a personal UPI ID for a “corporate” product, is the whole trick.

4. **Read fees, risks, lock-ins, exit rules, and complaint procedures.** A lock-in is the period you cannot leave, or can leave only at a cost. Why: these decide whether you can get out if the product is wrong for you, and where you go if something fails.

5. **Take time. Refuse secrecy, urgency, remote access, and guaranteed returns.** Why: honest products survive a night’s sleep and a second person’s questions. Guaranteed high market returns are not a feature. They are a warning.

Do not use a registration link or phone number supplied only by the seller as your verification source.

Harish was shown a certificate with a registration number. He opened [SEBI Investor](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/) himself on his own phone and searched the name. A similar name existed. It was not the same name, and it was not “approved” to take his UPI payment. He did not send the PAN copy. The window that “closed this week” was still closed the honest way: he walked away.

AI may help you create a checklist, but it cannot certify that a provider is genuine. Do not paste your PAN, Aadhaar, account number, or the seller’s full document into a public tool and ask “is this real?” For India, start with the relevant official resources from [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/), [SEBI Investor](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/), [IRDAI](https://irdai.gov.in/), or [PFRDA](https://www.pfrda.org.in/).

This page cannot tell you that a product is suitable. It can tell you how to check that the name on the form exists where it claims to exist, before you share identity papers or money.

## Before you share a document or pay

Open the official register on a site you typed yourself. Search the exact legal name. If it is not there, or the name is only similar, stop. A genuine provider can wait while you check. A copied certificate cannot.
