# Understand insurance

Someone at the office, or a relative, or a person at the hospital desk, puts a form in front of you. “Everyone buys this.” The number per month looks small. What is less clear is what event this money is actually for, and what it will refuse to pay.

**Insurance** transfers a defined financial risk: you pay a regular amount so that, if a named event happens, the insurer may pay a named kind of cost. It does not remove every risk or guarantee every claim. The monthly or yearly amount you pay is the **premium**. It is the price of the cover, not a savings account and not a refund waiting to come back.

## Before buying

1. **Identify what event you need protection from.** Hospital bills, a vehicle accident, a house fire, the death of someone the household depends on. Why: a policy bought because it was on offer is a policy you cannot use when a different event arrives.

2. **Read who and what is covered.** Named people, named vehicles, named illnesses or accidents. Why: a relative who is not listed, or a vehicle that is not listed, is not “sort of included.”

3. **Check exclusions, waiting periods, limits, deductibles, and renewal conditions.** An **exclusion** is something the policy will not pay for. A **waiting period** is the time after you buy during which certain claims will not be paid. A **limit** is the maximum they will pay. A **deductible** (sometimes called a copay or excess) is the portion you still pay yourself when a claim is accepted. Renewal conditions say whether they can change the premium or refuse to continue. Why: these lines, not the brochure photograph, decide whether the policy helps.

4. **Understand the premium and what happens if payments stop.** When it is due, whether it can rise, and whether cover ends if you miss a payment. Why: a lapsed policy is not a delayed policy. It may be no policy.

5. **Disclose required information truthfully.** Existing illness, past claims, who will drive the vehicle. Why: a claim can be refused if the form you signed hid a fact they asked for. This is not a trick question. It is the record they will open later.

6. **Verify the insurer and intermediary independently.** The company that will pay, and the person or app selling it, should exist on the official register, under the same legal name. Why: a professional card and a policy-shaped PDF can be copied.

Do not rely only on a sales illustration, verbal promise, or AI summary. Read the official policy wording and ask for unclear points in writing.

Meena bought health cover after a colleague’s surgery. She did not see the waiting period for a condition her family already knew about. Months later a claim for that condition was not paid, because the wording she had not read had always said so. The lesson is not “never buy.” It is: the waiting period and the exclusions are the policy.

AI can simplify general terminology after private details are removed, but it should not decide which policy you buy. Do not paste your full proposal form, medical history, or policy number into a public tool. Indian consumers can consult [IRDAI](https://irdai.gov.in/) for policyholder information and official resources. **IRDAI** is the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, the body that oversees insurers in this country.

This page is not personal advice. It cannot tell you which cover you need. For that, use the official policy wording, IRDAI’s resources, and a qualified person who is licensed to sell or advise on insurance.

## Do this before the next sales conversation

Write one sentence: the event you actually want protection from. Take that sentence to the form. If the form is about a different event, you are being sold a different product.
