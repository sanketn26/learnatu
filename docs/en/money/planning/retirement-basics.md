# Start planning for retirement

A parent who stopped regular work still has a kitchen, medicines, and a rent or a house to keep. The pension or the savings that felt adequate on the last working day look smaller every year, not because anyone wasted them, but because prices moved.

**Retirement** planning means preparing for future living costs when regular work income reduces or stops. Starting small and consistently can be more practical than waiting for a perfect plan. You do not need a complete forecast to begin naming the pieces.

**Inflation** is the way the same rice, rent, and medicine cost more over the years. A monthly cost that is manageable today will not stay the same number in twenty-five years.

## Build the picture

Write what you know. Leave blanks. Blanks are information too — they are the questions to ask next.

- **Expected essential living and healthcare costs.** What the household needs to eat, stay housed, move around, and pay for care, in today’s rupees first.
- **Existing pensions, retirement accounts, savings, property, and debts.** Provident fund, NPS or other pension, bank savings, a house you might live in, loans that will still be there.
- **People who may depend on you.** Children still in education, a parent without their own cover, a spouse with little separate income.
- **Time remaining and how inflation may affect costs.** More years can mean more time to put money aside, and more years of rising prices.
- **Access, risk, fees, and tax treatment of any product considered.** When you can take the money, what it can lose, what you pay to stay in, and how withdrawals are treated. If you cannot explain those four in your own words, you do not yet understand the product.

## A realistic example

Someone earning ₹40,000 a month plans to retire in 25 years. If essential monthly costs today are ₹25,000, inflation alone could push that above ₹1,00,000 a month by retirement. Starting a small, consistent contribution now — even a modest amount — compounds over 25 years far better than waiting 10 years to start a larger one. The exact numbers depend on your own income, costs, and chosen products, but the principle holds: time matters more than the size of the first contribution.

That example is a sketch, not a forecast of *your* prices or *your* returns. Markets, health, work, and rules can all change.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Move retirement savings immediately because of a call, message, or urgent deadline.
    - Choose a product because of a guaranteed high-return promise — no legitimate pension product can guarantee market returns.
    - Sign paperwork you have not read in full, including fee structure and lock-in period.

A lock-in period is the time during which you cannot take the money out, or can take it out only with a penalty. Fees are not a footnote. Over twenty-five years they change the result.

AI can produce questions and scenarios, but it cannot know future returns, inflation, lifespan, regulation, or your complete needs. Do not paste account numbers, PAN, or nomination details into a public tool to “get a plan.” For official Indian pension information, consult [PFRDA](https://www.pfrda.org.in/); use a qualified professional for individual decisions when necessary. **PFRDA** is the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, which oversees pension products such as the National Pension System in India.

This page cannot tell you how much to set aside or which product to use. It can tell you what picture to build before anyone sells you one.

## Do this week

Write today’s essential monthly costs — the same list you would need if work stopped. Keep the paper. That number is the start of a retirement plan, even if the first contribution is still small.
