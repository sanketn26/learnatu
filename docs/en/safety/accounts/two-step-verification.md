# Two-step verification

A password is one lock. **Two-step verification** (sometimes called 2FA or two-factor authentication) adds a second lock: after the password, the service asks for something only you should have — a code on this phone, a tap on a prompt, or a **passkey** stored on the device.

If a scammer steals or guesses the password, they still should not get in. That is why this is worth turning on first for email, banking, WhatsApp, and any account you would hate to lose.

## Set it up safely

1. Open the app yourself. Do not follow a “turn on 2FA” link in a message. That link may be a fake page collecting the very codes meant to protect you.
2. Look for **Security** or **Account**.
3. Prefer a passkey or an **authenticator app** (a small app that generates changing codes) when the service offers one. A code by SMS is still much better than password-only. SMS can be stolen if someone takes over your SIM, but it stops a huge number of casual break-ins.
4. When the service shows **recovery codes** or backup codes, write them down and keep them offline — a drawer, not the same email inbox. Those codes are spare keys. Anyone who has them can get in.
5. Review the recovery phone number, recovery email, and the list of signed-in devices. Remove what you do not recognise.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Share an authentication code, an “Is this you?” approval, a QR setup image, or a recovery code with anyone.
    - Follow a setup link sent in a message instead of opening the app yourself.
    - Approve a login notification you did not request.

> If an unexpected login approval appears, deny it. Then change the password from the official app or website. Someone is trying the first lock and hoping you will hold the second one open.
