# Lost or stolen phone

The phone is gone. The accounts on it are not gone yet — unless someone unlocks it. Act quickly, but do not let a second stranger “help you find it.”

## Do this now

1. From another phone or computer you trust, use Apple’s Find My or Google’s Find My Device. Type those names yourself. Do not tap a “track my phone” link in a message.
2. Mark the phone as lost and lock it remotely if the service allows it. That is a screen lock from a distance.
3. Call your mobile provider (Airtel, Jio, Vi, BSNL — the number on a bill or the official app) and ask them to block the SIM. The SIM is the small card that gives the phone its number. Blocking it stops OTPs and calls from arriving on the stolen handset.
4. Call your bank if banking or UPI apps could be opened — especially if the phone had a weak lock, or none.
5. Change your primary email password, then other important passwords, from a trusted device.
6. Report the theft to local police when it is a theft, not just a sofa-cushion loss. A complaint number helps with the bank.

If you are sure the phone will not come back, you can erase it remotely. Know that tracking often stops after an erase. That is a trade: data gone, location gone.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Reply to a message claiming someone found your phone and asking for your PIN, OTP, or password.
    - Click a “login” or “track my phone” link sent by a stranger.
    - Delay locking the phone and blocking the SIM while you decide what to do.

Apple and Google’s finding tools never need you to tell a stranger your PIN, OTP, or password. Anyone who asks for those to “locate” the phone is not locating it. They are opening it.
