# Impersonation, cloned voices, and “digital arrest”

The voice sounds like your son. The caller ID says “truecaller: State Bank.” A video call shows a person in a police uniform. A WhatsApp profile uses a photo you recognise.

None of that is proof. Photos are copied. Voices can be recorded and, increasingly, generated. Uniforms can be bought. Caller ID can be faked. **Impersonation** means pretending to be someone you would obey or rush to help.

## Verify using another channel

1. End the unexpected call. You are allowed to hang up. A real emergency will still be an emergency in five minutes, and a real relative will pick up when you dial *them*.
2. Call the person back on a number you already saved — not the number that just rang.
3. Ask a question only the real person would know. “What did we eat last Sunday?” is better than “Is this you?”
4. If they claimed to be a bank, the police, or a government office, call the organisation on a number you find yourself: the back of the card, the official `.gov.in` website, a local station you already know.

No legitimate authority keeps you under **“digital arrest”** — a video call where you must sit, stay silent, and transfer money to “prove innocence.” Indian police do not run court from WhatsApp. Urgent secrecy, threats, and “do not tell your family” are the plot. They are not the law.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Transfer money over a call to “prove your innocence” or stay under “digital arrest.”
    - Share your screen or move to a private room because a caller pressures you to.
    - Trust a familiar voice, face, or caller ID as proof of identity.

If a call is already in progress and any of this is happening, hang up now. Walk to another person in the house. Then verify. The pause is the whole defence.
