# QR codes and payment scams

A **QR code** is that square patch of dots. Your camera reads it and either opens a website or prepares a UPI payment. Scanning is not the same as receiving money. Often it is the opposite: you are lining up a payment *from* your account.

At a shop, a printed QR at the counter can be fine if you read the name and amount before you enter your PIN. A QR someone *sends you in a chat* to “receive a refund” is a different object wearing the same face.

## Rules that prevent loss

- You normally do **not** enter a UPI PIN to receive money. PIN is for sending. If they want your PIN, they want your money.
- Read the amount and the “paying to” name on *your* screen before you approve. Not on their screenshot. Not on their SMS.
- Confirm received money inside your own bank or UPI app, on a completed transaction, not on a caller’s screen-share.
- Never scan a code sent by someone claiming it will issue a refund.

If a buyer says they “accidentally paid extra,” look in your own app before you return anything. Fake success screens and edited screenshots are cheap to make and very good at creating hurry.

!!! danger "Never"
    - Enter your UPI PIN to receive money.
    - Scan a code sent by someone claiming it will issue a refund.
    - Trust a screenshot, SMS, or a caller’s screen as proof of payment.
