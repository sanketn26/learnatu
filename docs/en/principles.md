# Guiding principles

Eight rules. They are not slogans. They are the pause you take when a message, a call, or a confident answer is trying to hurry you.

Read them once. You will start to recognise the shape of a scam before it reaches your money.

---

1. **Pause before you tap, paste, or pay.**
   Your phone is designed for speed. Scams are designed for that speed. One extra second — lock the screen, put the phone down, ask someone next to you — is worth more than an hour of trying to reverse a payment.

2. **Secrets stay off chat.**
   Some information is like a key. Once it is in a message, a form, or an AI chat box, you cannot take it back. Never type an OTP, a UPI PIN, the three-digit CVV on the back of a card, a password, a photo of Aadhaar or PAN, backup codes, a customer list, or an unpublished contract into any box you did not open yourself.

3. **Official app and official number only.**
   Do not install an app from a link in a message. Open the Play Store or App Store yourself and search. If you need to call a bank, turn the card over and use the number printed there, or type the bank’s website yourself. The number that just called you is not “the bank.”

4. **Treat every confident answer — human or AI — as a draft. Check it.**
   ChatGPT, Google, a WhatsApp forward, a well-meaning relative, and a person in uniform on a video call can all be wrong. For anything that would change your money, your health, your job, or your child’s schoolwork, look it up on an official site or ask a qualified person.

5. **If someone rushes you, it is probably a scam.**
   “Pay in the next ten minutes or you will be arrested.” “Your account will be blocked today.” “This job offer expires tonight.” Real banks, police, and employers do not run your life on a countdown. Urgency is a tool. Slow down. The genuine thing will still be there in an hour.

6. **Share the minimum. The default is “do not upload.”**
   Before you send a photo, a document, or a piece of information, ask whether the other person truly needs it. A delivery app needs an address. It does not need your Aadhaar. An AI tool can rewrite a message without the customer’s phone number in it.

7. **You can refuse. Closing the chat is a valid action.**
   You do not owe a reply to a stranger on WhatsApp. You do not have to answer every question an AI asks. You do not have to stay on a call because the caller said “do not hang up.” Putting the phone down is not rudeness. It is control.

8. **Teach the person next to you the same rule.**
   A scam that failed on you will be tried on a parent, a colleague, a customer. Share one rule at a time — “nobody who asks for an OTP on a call is genuine” is enough for a whole family.
